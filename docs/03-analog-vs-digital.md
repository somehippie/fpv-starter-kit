# 3. Analog vs. Digital — Why Analog

This build deliberately chose **analog** (SteadyView-class) video over the two dominant digital systems, primarily on price.

| System | Video quality | Latency | Cost of entry | Ecosystem | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Analog (SteadyView-class) | Lower resolution, static-prone | Lowest | Cheapest goggles + VTX | Large, mature, highly interchangeable parts market | Chosen for this build |
| DJI O3/O4 (digital) | High resolution, clean | Low, but higher than analog | Expensive goggles + air unit, DJI-locked ecosystem | Smaller, proprietary | Rejected — digital-only, no analog fallback, higher buy-in |
| HDZero (digital) | High resolution | Low | Mid-to-high | Growing but smaller than analog | Considered for goggles (see [Goggle Choice](04-goggle-choice.md)); analog support exists only as a secondary/adapter feature |

## The price argument

Analog goggles and VTX/receiver hardware are meaningfully cheaper than either DJI's proprietary digital system or HDZero's digital line, and secondhand analog gear is widely available given how long the format has been the hobby standard. For a first real build, that combination — lower buy-in, bigger parts market, easier and cheaper crash recovery — outweighed the resolution gain of going digital from day one.

## Other factors

- **DJI Goggles 2** were ruled out entirely: digital-only (O3/O4), with no analog compatibility at all.
- **HDZero Goggle 2** were a real contender on the goggle side (see next section) but treat analog as a secondary feature rather than a native mode, and carried cost/availability concerns.
- Digital systems remain a reasonable upgrade path later — this decision is about the best low-cost entry point, not a permanent ceiling.
